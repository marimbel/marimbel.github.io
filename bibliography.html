Reference

Annotated Bibliography

Listed in order of first appearance. Reliability: High peer-reviewed or primary technical source · Medium–High preprint or vendor documentation.

[1] A. Tharatipyakul, T. Srikaewsiew, S. Pongnumkul, "Deep learning-based human body pose estimation in providing feedback for physical movement: A review," Heliyon, vol. 10, no. 17, e36589, Aug. 2024. ScienceDirect · PubMed

Synopsis: A systematic review of 45 articles on systems that estimate body pose, assess a movement, and give the user feedback. It finds that pose estimation is mostly done with CNNs, that assessment ranges from formulas and rules to machine learning, and that feedback is mostly visual. It names the handling of erroneous feedback as an open question. I used it to frame why a separate stabilization layer matters (Sections 1 and 8).

Reliability: High Peer-reviewed, open-access review in an Elsevier/Cell Press journal, indexed in PubMed. The authors are researchers in Thailand working on pose-based feedback systems. As a review, it summarizes others' results rather than testing filters itself.

[2] V. Bazarevsky, I. Grishchenko, K. Raveendran, T. Zhu, F. Zhang, M. Grundmann, "BlazePose: On-device Real-time Body Pose tracking," CVPR Workshop on Computer Vision for Augmented and Virtual Reality, 2020. arXiv:2006.10204

Synopsis: Describes the lightweight network behind MediaPipe Pose: 33 body keypoints for one person at over 30 fps on a Pixel 2 phone, trained with both heatmaps and coordinate regression, and run as a detector–tracker pipeline where the previous pose guides the next crop. I used it to explain how video pose estimation works and why outputs are still frame-to-frame noisy (Section 1.1).

Reliability: High Workshop paper at CVPR, the top computer vision conference, written by the Google engineers who built the model. Primary source for MediaPipe's design, though written by the vendor.

[3] Google AI Edge, "Pose landmark detection guide" (MediaPipe Pose Landmarker), and MediaPipe Pose solution documentation. Accessed Oct. 2026. ai.google.dev

Synopsis: Official documentation for the MediaPipe Pose Landmarker: 33 landmarks, image-normalized landmarks and metric world landmarks, IMAGE / VIDEO / LIVE_STREAM running modes, tracking-confidence settings, and lite/full/heavy models. I used it for the meaning of landmark fields and confidence scores (Section 2) and to write the extraction script.

Reliability: Medium–High Vendor documentation: authoritative for how the API behaves, but not peer-reviewed and it changes between versions. It does not document internal smoothing details.

[4] R. Ullah, I. Asghar, R. Nawaz, C. Stacey, S. Akbar, P. Bishop, "A real time action scoring system for movement analysis and feedback in physical therapy using human pose estimation," Scientific Reports, vol. 15, art. 44784, Dec. 2025. Nature · PMC

Synopsis: A MediaPipe-based physical-therapy system that computes joint angles with the cosine rule, range of motion, angular velocity by central differences, and similarity to a reference movement with dynamic time warping and normalized cross-correlation; reps are found from peaks and troughs. It uses a fixed bounding box over the clip to reduce jitter and reports joint-angle errors around 1.6–3.2% versus clinicians and rep-count error around 7.5%. I used it as the main application example (Sections 1, 2 and 8).

Reliability: High Peer-reviewed article in a Nature Portfolio open-access journal. It is an applied systems paper; its evaluation is on its own data and it does not compare temporal filters directly.

[5] A. Savitzky, M. J. E. Golay, "Smoothing and Differentiation of Data by Simplified Least Squares Procedures," Analytical Chemistry, vol. 36, no. 8, pp. 1627–1639, 1964. doi:10.1021/ac60214a047

Synopsis: The original paper showing that fitting a local polynomial by least squares in a sliding window is equivalent to convolution with fixed coefficients, giving fast smoothing and derivative estimates that preserve peak shape. I used it for the offline smoothing baseline (Sections 3.3 and 9).

Reliability: High A classic, heavily cited peer-reviewed paper from the American Chemical Society; the method is standard in signal processing libraries such as SciPy.

[6] G. Casiez, N. Roussel, D. Vogel, "1€ Filter: A Simple Speed-based Low-pass Filter for Noisy Input in Interactive Systems," in Proc. ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, Austin, TX, May 2012, pp. 2527–2530. Project page

Synopsis: Introduces the One Euro filter: an exponential low-pass filter whose cutoff frequency increases with the smoothed speed of the signal, removing jitter at low speed and lag at high speed with two tunable parameters. The project page gives reference implementations and a step-by-step tuning procedure. Basis of Section 4 and the Filter Lab.

Reliability: High Peer-reviewed paper at CHI, the leading human–computer interaction conference; the authors are HCI researchers (Univ. of Lille / Inria, Univ. of Waterloo). Widely reused in AR/VR and pose tracking.

[7] R. E. Kalman, "A New Approach to Linear Filtering and Prediction Problems," Journal of Basic Engineering, vol. 82, no. 1, pp. 35–45, 1960. doi:10.1115/1.3662552

Synopsis: The original paper on the Kalman filter, formulating optimal recursive estimation of a linear system's state from noisy measurements. Cited for the origin of the method in Section 5.

Reliability: High Foundational peer-reviewed paper (ASME journal). Highly mathematical; I relied on [8] for the practical form of the equations.

[8] G. Welch, G. Bishop, "An Introduction to the Kalman Filter," Technical Report TR 95-041, Dept. of Computer Science, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, 1995 (updated 2006). PDF

Synopsis: A short, widely used tutorial giving the discrete Kalman filter's predict ("time update") and correct ("measurement update") equations, the meaning of Q and R, and tuning advice. Section 5's equations and Figure 5.1 follow its notation.

Reliability: High University technical report, not journal-reviewed, but written by tracking researchers at UNC and cited thousands of times as a standard introduction.

[9] X. Hu, G. Ye, "Learning from Watching: Scalable Extraction of Manipulation Trajectories from Human Videos," arXiv preprint, Dec. 2025. arXiv:2512.00024

Synopsis: Extracts hand, tool and object trajectories from human videos for robot learning. A foundation model picks task-relevant keypoints, a pretrained point tracker (LocoTrack) follows them, bidirectional tracking with consistency filtering removes unreliable tracks, and interpolation fills short occlusions and missing detections. I used its consistency check and interpolation as examples for Section 6.

Reliability: Medium–High Recent arXiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed as far as I could find. Its domain (manipulation) differs from exercise, so I used it only for general trajectory-cleaning ideas.

[10] A. Eghbalian, K. Desai, "Pose-to-Biomechanics: Bridging 3D Human Pose Estimation and Biomechanical Attribute Prediction," arXiv preprint, Jul. 2026. arXiv:2607.08725

Synopsis: Proposes BioModule, a lightweight temporal transformer attached after any 3D pose estimator that predicts 17 kinematic, kinetic and neuromuscular attributes from skeleton sequences, trained on about 520k frames aligned between Human3.6M and musculoskeletal simulation labels. Evaluating seven pose estimators shows how upstream pose errors carry into biomechanical predictions. Used in Sections 7, 10.2 and 10.5.

Reliability: Medium–High Very recent preprint from the University of Texas at San Antonio; not yet peer-reviewed. Results are on a lab dataset rather than real exercise video.

[11] A. Zeng, L. Yang, X. Ju, J. Li, J. Wang, Q. Xu, "SmoothNet: A Plug-and-Play Network for Refining Human Poses in Videos," in Proc. European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2022. arXiv:2112.13715

Synopsis: A temporal-only refinement network that is placed after any pose estimator. A motion-aware fully-connected network models long-range temporal patterns of each joint (ignoring inter-joint relations) to remove jitter, especially in occluded or rare-pose frames, and transfers across backbones and datasets. Used as the main example of learned smoothing (Section 10.4).

Reliability: High Peer-reviewed paper at ECCV, a top computer vision conference, with public code.

[12] D. Wei, Z. Shen, Y. Xia, G. Pavlakos, Y. Shen, X. Zhou, "Natural Human Motion Recovery by Aligning High-Order Temporal Dynamics from Monocular Videos," in Proc. IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2026. arXiv:2605.26879

Synopsis: Introduces HTD-Refine, a post-processing framework for monocular human motion recovery. A temporal transformer (PVA-Net) predicts per-joint 2D positions, 3D velocities and 3D accelerations from video; these act as soft constraints in a global optimization that reduces both jitter and over-smoothing. It improves existing pipelines on in-the-wild benchmarks. Used in Section 10.4 as the state of the art on the smoothness problem.

Reliability: High Accepted at CVPR 2026 (listed as an oral and best paper candidate). Authors include researchers from Zhejiang University and UT Austin. Very recent, so independent follow-up results are not yet available.
